How Webb Sees the First Galaxies

1 wk. ago
Open post🔔 Remind me＋•••

✎ Add note

The pointWebb can see galaxies from the early universe because their light has been stretched into infrared on its 13-billion-year trip to us.
Topics
Redshift; how Webb works
Mirror
6.5 m, 18 gold segments
Distance from Earth
1.5 million km
  • Light from far galaxies takes billions of years to arrive, so you see them as they were
  • The universe expanding stretches that light into infrared, which our eyes can't see
  • A sunshield the size of a tennis court keeps the telescope cold enough to catch faint heat

  1. Looking back in time

    ▶ 0:03

    Distant light is old light, so far away also means long ago

  2. Why it has to be infrared

    ▶ 0:21

    Expansion stretches visible light into longer wavelengths

    Hubble mostly sees visible light, Webb is built for infrared

  3. A mirror made of gold

    ▶ 0:44

    18 hexagon segments unfolded in space into one 6.5 m mirror

  4. Parked 1.5 million km out

    ▶ 1:12

    Webb orbits a quiet point beyond the Moon

    The shield blocks the Sun, Earth and Moon at once
